You see the word RED printed in blue ink. The answer is blue. Your mouth still wants to say red.

That tiny stumble is the whole test. The instructions take one sentence to explain and a surprising amount of effort to follow: name the colour of the ink, not the word, as quickly as you can without guessing.

This guide walks through taking our [free Stroop test](/#test) from the first screen to the results. It takes about two minutes, so you might want to open it in another tab and follow along.

## Before you start

A minute of setup is worth it, because the test is sensitive enough to pick up every distraction.

**Find somewhere quiet.** A phone buzzing next to you or someone talking in the room will show up in your times.

**Use a keyboard if you can.** Phones and tablets work fine, but a physical keyboard usually gives steadier timing and your fingers settle into place quicker.

**Close the noise.** Mute notifications and close tabs that might play a sound. If you switch to another tab during the test, it pauses itself and redoes that trial, but it's better not to.

**Wear your screen glasses** if you normally use them. Squinting at colours slows you down.

**If you're colour blind,** especially red-green, pick the colour-vision friendly level. It uses blue, yellow and white on a grey background, which most people with red-green colour blindness can tell apart easily.

## Step 1: pick a level and do the practice round

When the test loads, you choose a level.

![Easy, Standard and Hard level buttons](/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Difficulty-Selector.webp "Pick a level before you begin.")

- **Easy:** 20 trials with four colours. A quick first look.
- **Standard:** 40 trials with four colours. This gives the most reliable score and is the one to use if you want to track yourself over time.
- **Hard:** 60 trials with six colours, adding purple and orange, and a limit of 1.5 seconds per word. Answers that come too late count as wrong.
- **Colour-vision friendly:** 30 trials with blue, yellow and white.

If it's your first time, go with Standard.

Then leave the practice round switched on. It's six trials that don't count. You get feedback after each one, so you learn where the keys are before the timing matters. Skipping it usually means your first few scored answers are slow while you hunt for keys, and that spoils your numbers.

![A practice trial showing the word BLUE in red ink](/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practice-Round-Screenshot.webp "Practice trials give feedback and don't count towards your score.")

## Step 2: answer each word

Here's the rule again, because almost everyone slips on it at least once: **answer with the ink colour, never the word.** If GREEN appears in red ink, the answer is red.

Each trial starts with a small **+** in the middle of the screen. Keep your eyes there. After a short, slightly random pause the word appears, and the clock starts.

![Keyboard keys R, B, G and Y](/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Keyboard-Key-Map.webp "R for red, B for blue, G for green, Y for yellow.")

On a keyboard the keys are the first letters of the colours: **R** red, **B** blue, **G** green, **Y** yellow. Hard adds **P** for purple and **O** for orange, and the colour-vision friendly level uses **W** for white. The keys are shown on the buttons, so glance once and then trust your fingers. On a phone, tap the buttons instead.

You'll also see some ordinary words, like LAMP or DOOR, in a colour. These are neutral trials. Answer with their ink colour the same way.

You'll feel a little drag on the clashing words. That drag is the [Stroop effect](/stroop-effect-explained/), and it's completely normal. People have shown it in every version of the task since 1935.

Go quickly, but don't guess. The test counts both speed and accuracy, and an answer faster than 200 ms is treated as a guess and marked wrong. A steady, focused pace gets a better result than a frantic one.

## Step 3: read your report

When the last trial finishes, your report appears straight away. It works everything out in your browser, and nothing is sent anywhere.

![Results panel showing accuracy, reaction time and interference](/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Live-Accuracy-Bar.webp "The report turns your answers into a few clear numbers.")

You'll see:

- **Interference:** how much slower you were on clashing words than on matching ones. This is the main number.
- **Relative interference:** the same gap as a percentage of your matching-word speed. Fairer if you're comparing across ages or devices.
- **Accuracy:** the share of trials you got right.
- **Typical response:** the median time of your correct answers.
- **Speed + accuracy:** a combined score that stops fast guessing from looking good.

There are also two charts. One shows your median time for each trial type. The other plots every single trial, so you can spot a slow patch or a run of mistakes.

Our guide to [the interference score](/stroop-interference-score/) explains the main number in detail, and [what counts as a good Stroop score](/good-stroop-test-score/) helps you put it in context.

## Common mistakes

**Reading the word.** The biggest one. It creeps back the moment your attention drifts.

**Rushing.** Fast and wrong isn't a good score. If your accuracy falls much below 90%, slow down a little.

**Testing when wrecked.** At 2 a.m., after three coffees and a bad day, the result tells you more about your evening than about your attention.

**Retaking it over and over.** Ten runs in a row will make you faster at the game, which isn't the same thing. Once you're practised, it stops measuring what it's meant to.

## Tips for a cleaner score

- Do the practice round until the keys feel automatic.
- Rest a hand lightly on the keys so you're not hunting for them mid-trial.
- Sit up and give it your full attention for the two minutes it runs.
- For a fair picture, take it on a few different days rather than a few different minutes. Your browser keeps a short history so you can compare.

## A quick reminder

This is a learning tool, not a medical test. A Stroop score can't diagnose ADHD, dementia or anything else, whatever the number says. If you're worried about your focus or memory, talk to a qualified clinician. For the background on the task itself, see [what the Stroop test is](/what-is-the-stroop-test/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the instructions for the Stroop test?

Name the colour of the ink, not the word, as fast as you can without guessing. Press the matching key or tap the matching button. The test times each answer and reports your speed, accuracy and interference at the end.

### Do I name the word or the ink colour?

Always the ink. If BLUE is printed in yellow, the answer is yellow.

### How many trials are there?

It depends on the level: 20 on Easy, 40 on Standard, 60 on Hard and 30 on the colour-vision friendly level. There's also an optional six-trial practice round.

### Should I use a keyboard or tap?

A keyboard if you have one. Timing is a little steadier and your fingers find a rhythm faster. Tapping on a phone works well too.

### What are the words that aren't colours?

Those are neutral trials, words like LAMP or CHAIR in a coloured ink. They show your plain colour-naming speed, without a colour word getting in the way. Answer with the ink colour, just like the others.

### Which level should I choose?

Standard for your first proper go and for tracking over time. Easy for a quick try, Hard once you know the keys and want more of a challenge.

### What if I switch tabs by accident?

The test pauses and shows the same trial again when you come back, so a stray click doesn't ruin the timing.

### What happens after the last trial?

Your report appears straight away with your interference score, accuracy, typical response time and two charts. You can print it, save it as a PDF, download the raw data or share a link to your summary.
