Stroop Tester is a small, independent site with two parts: a free online version of the Stroop colour-word task, and a set of plain-English articles about attention, executive function and the research behind the test.

We built it because most online Stroop tests either hide how they score you or dress up rough numbers as clinical results. We wanted one that's quick, free, explains exactly what it measures, and is honest about what it can't tell you.

## How the test works

The test follows the standard computerised design used in research:

- Three trial types: matching words (RED in red), clashing words (RED in blue) and neutral words (LAMP in blue).
- A fixation cross before each word, with a randomly varied pause so you can't anticipate the next one.
- Times recorded with the browser's high-resolution clock, starting on the frame that draws the word.
- Scoring based on the **median** time of correct answers, with guesses under 200 ms and lapses over 3 seconds left out.

Everything is worked out in your browser. Your answers never reach our servers. You can download your raw trial data as a CSV file from the results screen if you'd like to check the numbers yourself.

## What we don't claim

- **This is not a diagnostic test.** It can't tell you whether you have ADHD, dementia, a brain injury or any other condition, and you shouldn't use it to make medical decisions.
- **Our result bands are rough guides,** based on the range of results commonly reported for keyboard and touch versions of the task. They aren't a validated normative sample, and we don't give percentiles, because we don't have a reference sample that would make them meaningful.
- **Online timing has limits.** Keyboards, touchscreens and displays add their own delays. The interference score cancels out most of that, but raw response times can't be compared fairly between devices.

## Editorial standards

Articles are written and reviewed by the Stroop Tester editorial team. We don't present them as medical advice, but we do work hard to get the science right:

- Factual claims are checked against primary research or reputable sources such as the CDC, NIMH, the American Psychiatric Association and peer-reviewed reviews. Key papers are named in the text so you can look them up.
- When the evidence is mixed, as it is for the Stroop test and ADHD, we say so.
- Illustrations that show made-up numbers are labelled as illustrations.
- Each article shows when it was first published and when it was last updated.

If you spot a mistake, please [tell us](/contact/). We'll correct it and update the date.

## Medical disclaimer

The content on this site is for general information and education only. It isn't a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. If you have concerns about your attention, memory or thinking, speak to your doctor or another qualified health professional.
